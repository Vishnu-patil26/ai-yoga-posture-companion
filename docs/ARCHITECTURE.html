System Architecture — base implementation (Vrikshasana) · Vishnu Patil

1Runtime pipeline — the life of one frame

Everything below runs 28 times a second on one CPU core. Timings are measured medians over 50 frames at 1000×667 (Intel i7-13xxx, no GPU).

Where the time goes. The neural network costs 13.9 ms; the whole scoring layer this project contributes costs 0.13 ms — roughly 1% of the budget. The pipeline is bounded entirely by MediaPipe, which is what makes a 30 fps camera comfortable on a laptop with no GPU.

2Module structure

Thirteen modules, ~4,200 lines. The dependency graph is deliberately shallow: no module imports more than three siblings, and the asana definitions import nothing at all.

ModuleLinesResponsibility
landmarks.py143Wraps the MediaPipe Tasks API. The rest of the code never sees a mediapipe type.
filters.py89One-Euro filter: heavy smoothing when still, almost none when moving fast.
angles.py62Joint geometry in image space, written so asanas read like a teacher's cues.
asanas.py262Pure declaration. Checks, targets, tolerances, variants. No logic, no imports.
evaluator.py336Measure the body; grade it joint by joint; pick the best-fitting variant.
state_machine.py155Hold timer with hysteresis and a grace window.
coach.py246The guided spoken entry into the pose, gated on measured body position.
feedback.py180Which cue, when to say it, and the text-to-speech worker thread.
overlay.py332Skeleton, HUD, framing panel, reference card.
reference.py150The ideal figure, shared by the on-screen card and the self-test.
calibration.py92Personal tolerances learned from the practitioner's own best attempt.
storage.py122SQLite practice log. Derived numbers only.
Why the graph matters. asanas.py holds no logic, so adding a second asana is one declaration and one fitting run — the engine does not change. And because reference.py feeds both the on-screen card and the test suite, the figure the practitioner is shown is by construction the figure the scorer accepts.

4Offline pipeline — where the reference angles come from

This runs once, not per frame. It is what makes the numbers in asanas.py defensible rather than guessed.

Why fit at all? No standard table of ideal joint angles per asana exists in the literature — the papers that do angle-based correction each derive their own from data and mostly never publish the numbers. Three fitted values contradicted the obvious guess: the hips sit 10° off level, not 0°; the folded knee closes to 30°, not 45°; the foot sits at 0.80 of the standing leg, not 0.70.

5Verification and delivery

PathWhat it proves
tools/selftest.py66 assertions on synthetic skeletons of known geometry — no camera, no dataset. Angles recover to within 0.6°; each wrong pose names the right fault; the hold timer rides out a 0.7 s wobble but ends on a 2 s loss.
tools/simulate_session.pyA scripted 90-second practice driven through the real evaluator, state machine, cue engine and logger. Asserts two holds complete.
tools/analyse.pyAny clip or photo → per-frame CSV of every joint. The harness for instructor-labelled ground truth (Stage 5).
bootstrap.pySelf-healing setup: every requirement is a check paired with a repair, looped until green. Runs the self-test as its final gate.
DockerfileRuns the self-test during the image build — a green image is a green engine.
Privacy, as built. No frame is ever written to disk; frames are decoded, measured and discarded. The practice log holds joint angles, deviations, scores, hold durations and cue text — nothing else — in a local SQLite file. Every stage runs on the machine; there is no network call at run time.
Scope. This is the asana-trainer track, complete, for one asana. The desk-posture and contactless-vitals tracks in the methodology are not built yet. The validation above proves the system identifies Vrikshasana against other asanas; it does not yet prove it grades a good Vrikshasana against a poor one — that needs instructor-labelled footage, for which the harness already exists.
